# Design notes

The decisions behind the details, and what changing them would involve. The overall design is in [design.md](design.md). Numbers marked "measured" come from an 85-minute, 23-file 4K ride filmed on a DJI Osmo Action 6, analyzed on an RTX 5090; see [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md).

## Files, recordings and time

- **Files are identified by size plus three 1 MiB samples** (start, middle, end). This is fast on large footage and survives moving files. It is an identity, not an integrity check. A file whose content changed at the same path is treated as new footage; if the new content can't be read, the old recording and its decisions are kept, marked as changed, and can't be rendered until the file is fixed.
- **Recording ids are never reused**, so old edit revisions can't point at different footage.
- **Cameras are told apart by the folder their files were copied into**, as a path from the footage root (`Anna/DJI_001` and `Ben/DJI_001` are two cameras), and with several footage roots, by root as well. Reading serial numbers would be better and is on the roadmap.
- **A phone's videos and photos are one camera** when its videos name their make and model (iPhones and Android phones do), so the zone in a photo, or in an iPhone video, applies to all of them: a recording without a zone takes the zone of the same camera's nearest recording within two days. Cameras whose videos don't say what they are stay separate from their photos.
- **Time evidence, best first:** GPS time; metadata with a zone offset; metadata plus a zone from the file name (DJI) or from the camera's setting in `reelsift.toml`; a time without a zone, read as local wall-clock time and converted once the camera's zone is set; the file's modified time as a last resort, flagged on the timeline.
- **GoPro** writes its local clock into `creation_time` (checked against GPS on HERO5, HERO6 and MAX files: 7 to 8 hours apart on footage from California), so it is read as local time. On models with GPS, the first telemetry second with a fix gives UTC (GPSU, or GPS9 on the HERO11 and later; the telemetry is searched up to 20 minutes in, in any chapter of the recording), and comparing the two gives the zone; a clock that was never set (a Fusion was 82 days off) leaves the zone unknown but keeps the GPS time. The model comes from the telemetry, or from the firmware code (HD5 is a HERO5) when the telemetry only says "Camera".
- **Cycliq** writes when each 5-minute segment *ended*, on its local clock (the same clock is burned into the picture), so a segment starts its duration earlier. Segments whose numbers follow each other and whose times meet within 5 s are one recording; locked `LCK_` segments keep their number and join in.
- **Chapters with consecutive numbers are one recording**; a missing chapter splits it, since the time after it can't be known from the chapters before it, and GoPro chapters must also start where the previous one ended. A chapter copied in later is added to the end of the recording it continues (keeping its id and review decisions; analysis is redone), or, if it belongs in the middle, the recording is made again with a warning.
- **A local time in a file name** (`20200705_150405.mp4`, `VID_20200705_150405.mp4`) next to a UTC `creation_time` gives the zone, as DJI file names do. A zero difference gives nothing, since such names may be in UTC.
- **Anchors correct whole cameras**, solved by least squares per group of linked cameras; disagreement is reported per anchor.
- **Recording time counts from the first video frame.** Files whose audio starts before or after the video are cut from the right frames, and their sound keeps its timing.

## Analysis

- **Analysis times are seconds as floating point** (speech, observations, candidates, edits). Exact fractions are kept where frames matter: chapter and proxy offsets and clocks. Cut points are snapped to the source frame grid when rendering.
- **Per-second observations:** camera motion, scene activity and shake from optical flow at 5 fps on a 160-pixel proxy; sharpness, brightness, crushed shadows and clipped highlights at 1 fps; scene change as the embedding distance between consecutive seconds; loudness and the share of each second covered by words. Every sample is placed by its own time in the recording.
- **Embeddings** are float16 `.npy` files per recording (about 12 MB per hour of footage), written through a temporary file. A missing or damaged file makes analysis redo that recording. Embeddings made by a different model than the current one (after switching presets) are ignored until analysis catches up.
- **The prompt bank is generic** (scenery, landmarks, people, faces, kids, action, water, food, animals, sky, city, plain road, ground, blocked view). Its text embeddings are computed during analysis, so selection never loads a model.
- **The vision model describes fixed 6-second blocks** (six frames at 1 fps, long side 448 pixels), not candidates, so it never re-runs when candidates change. On the large preset every block is described (measured: 7 minutes for the 85-minute ride, all answers valid).
- **Tags** come from a fixed list of 24 (scenery, landmark, people, conversation, laughter, kids, animal, water, food, city, indoors, sky, action, sport, vehicle, walking, riding, stop, road, trail, crowd, celebration, close-up, boring), plus an interest score from 1 to 10. Answers are checked strictly; an unusable answer is stored as unknown. Interest scores bunch at 6 to 8, so they are only useful after per-trip normalization.
- **Models load only when their stage has work**, one stage at a time. Running out of GPU memory stops the stage at once and suggests the next smaller preset; finished recordings are kept.
- **Jobs are resumable.** Each stage works out what is missing from what is actually saved, so a result deleted since an interrupted run is made again.

## Candidates, scoring and selection

- **Candidates are 4-second windows every 2 seconds plus speech phrases**, each with a 3-second handle on both sides. Decisions refer to recording intervals, not candidate ids, so candidates can be rebuilt freely.
- **Scoring is a weighted sum of robust z-scores** (median and interquartile range across the trip, clipped at ±3, unknown counts as 0). Modes: `combined` (default), `motion` and `embedding`. The default weights were set by judgment and haven't been tuned against labelled footage yet; `reelsift eval` with a `labels.toml` exists for that.
- **Selection is greedy with penalties** for clips near each other in one recording (within 30 s), clips that look alike (embedding cosine above 0.88), parts of the trip already covered, and a second angle of a moment already picked. Penalties scale with the spread of the scores, so one strong preference can't take over the whole edit (measured: a strongly preferred landmark went from 15 of 36 clips to 8).
- **Clip length grows with edit length:** about 2 s at 30 s, 4 s at 3 min, 7 s at 10 min. Speech clips may run longer so sentences aren't cut, and cut points move out of words.
- **Moments across cameras** are checked pair by pair (another camera, overlapping in real time and looking alike). Not yet tested on real multi-camera footage.
- **Photos** last 2 to 4 seconds on screen with a 6% zoom. Bursts (same camera, within 3 seconds of each other, embedding cosine 0.92 or more) count once; the sharpest is used.

## Preferences

- **One set per edit length**, each with a style: "preview" (coverage penalty 0.15, speech clips as short as others), "highlights" (0.5, as before) or "story" (1.5 with a finer division of the trip, speech up to 15 s). By default the shortest edit is a preview, the longest a story, and any in between are highlights. Clip lengths can be set per edit as a shortest and longest duration; otherwise they follow the edit length (about 2 s at 30 s, 4 s at 3 min, 7 s at 10 min).
- **Drafts.** After analysis, the language model gets up to 90 of its own scene descriptions (half the most interesting, half spread over the day), with times and interest, and writes two to four sentences per edit in its style. Each draft is read into sliders like typed text, but never excludes footage outright. A draft is written only for edits without a description of their own; `reelsift draft` and "Suggest again" replace one on purpose (keeping its clip lengths). Measured on the test ride: 62 s for three drafts, including loading the model.
- **Reading descriptions** asks the model to set only what the text clearly asks for (usually two to six settings) and at most a few searches; on long drafts it otherwise invented settings the text never mentioned.
- **Sliders from −3 to +3** over a fixed vocabulary (the measured signals, the prompt bank, the vision tags and interest), plus free-text searches answered from stored embeddings, plus optional "leave out" exclusions. A slider step adds 0.6 to a feature's weight; a search step adds 0.8.
- **Preference text is read by the vision model's language side** when the GPU allows, in any language; anything unusable falls back to an English keyword matcher. The first request takes about 25 s while the models load into the review server; after that, applying preferences takes under a second (measured). The model stays loaded until the app closes.
- **Search embeddings are cached** in the trip database, and new ones are computed before the review lock is taken, so the app stays responsive while a model loads.

## Review

- **Edits are independent.** Each length has its own decisions; nothing ties the 30 s edit to the clips of the 3 min edit. Earlier versions nested the edits (each inside the next longer one); opening such a trip gives every edit its own copy of the keeps and adds that applied to it, so its edits stay as they were.
- **Drop** removes footage from this edit, or from every edit ("everywhere"), and keeps it out of later selections there. Dropped footage is cut out of kept clips rather than removing them whole. A keep or add made after a drop overrides it for its own interval; a drop made after a keep always wins, even if restoring a revision switches the keep back on.
- **Add** puts footage into this edit; adding next to a clip joins them unless dropped footage lies between.
- **Trim** changes only this edit.
- **Nothing reshuffles on its own.** Only Select again (fresh choice, decisions kept) and Refill (keep every current clip, add until full) choose new clips.
- **Undo** switches back exactly the decisions a change switched off or created, and restores the edits that change applied to. Restoring a revision brings back that edit and its own decisions as they were; footage dropped from every edit since stays out.
- **Approval is a marker**, not a gate: rendering uses the revision on screen. Render and export are refused if the edit changed since it was shown.
- **The app** runs on 127.0.0.1, rejects requests whose Host or Origin isn't the app, and serves media only for recordings and outputs the trip knows. Responses that arrive out of order are dropped, so an older edit never replaces a newer one on screen. Previews play the proxies, with small pauses at cuts.

## Sound, music and rendering

- **Rendering cuts each clip from the original into an intermediate file, then joins them without re-encoding.** Long edits can resume, and commands stay small; the cost is hard cuts only. The output frame rate is the standard rate covering most of the edit.
- **Encoding falls back automatically:** GPU decode and encode, then CPU decode, then x264. Cancelling a render stops its processes and starts no fallback.
- **Music is optional and off by default:** generated for each edit, or your own files (played in order and repeated to cover the edit).
- **Generated music comes from ACE-Step 1.5** (code and models MIT; its model cards say its output may be used commercially), installed by `reelsift music install` into its own environment from a pinned source archive and pinned model revisions, because its PyTorch and transformers versions differ from Reelsift's. A small worker script drives it over JSON lines; generation never touches the network once installed. Measured on an RTX 5090: about 25 s to load, 16 GB at peak, 3 minutes of music in about 30 s; the requested tempo is kept; no vocals with `[Instrumental]` in the prompt (checked with Whisper on every test track).
- **What is asked for:** the genre (presets with a tempo range, or free words), the mood, intensity words from the edit's style, a few words about its shape from its motion over time ("builds up gradually to a big finish"), "sparse, leaves room for voices" when it is mostly speech, and an ending. The model follows the shape loosely, so two tracks are made and the one whose loudness best follows the edit's energy plays; the others stay available.
- **Endings:** tracks are asked for a little longer than the edit (they end on their own 1-4 s early); the music then stops at the edit's end with a fade of a few beats, or keeps its own ending when it lands there. Short tracks ask for a final hit, without which they end early.
- **Every edit revision has its soundtrack** (which music, its levels, how it was fitted); a new revision inherits it, and restoring an old revision brings back its music too. Generated tracks are kept in the trip's `music/` folder; `reelsift clean` never deletes them, `reelsift music prune` deletes those no revision uses. Music made for an edit that changed meanwhile is kept but not played.
- **Beats** come from a built-in tracker (NumPy): tempo from the onset envelope's autocorrelation, refined between frames, beats by dynamic programming, and a confidence (the autocorrelation at the beat period: 0.6-0.9 for steady beats, near 0 for ambient music). For generated music a grid near the requested tempo is tried too. Music without a clear beat isn't snapped to.
- **Cuts snap to beats during selection**, and on request ("fit cuts to the beat"), at most 0.4 s per cut and 1 s in all, never inside a word, only for clips you haven't locked, and within each clip's handles. Fitting always starts from the edit as it was before, so trying another track doesn't add up.
- **The mix:** the clips' own sound at full level where people speak and at the background level elsewhere when music plays; the music at its level, dropping while people speak. The defaults (music −6 dB, background −9.1 dB, drop −12.4 dB) are the levels Reelsift always used; each edit can have its own. Generated tracks are first brought to −20 LUFS, so they sit at the same level. Without music the clips' sound is left alone. Loudness is normalized in two passes to −16 LUFS with true peaks under −1.5 dBTP. Silent edits are left as they are.
- **One heavy GPU user at a time:** analysis, drafting, the review app's models and music generation take a machine-wide lock (released if a process dies); the review app frees its models before music is generated.
- **9:16 edits crop** at a per-clip position set in the app; photos are cropped the same way.
- **HDR (HLG/PQ) is tone-mapped to SDR Rec.709**, and every frame is tagged BT.709 explicitly. Variable-frame-rate clips seek to exact times rather than a frame grid.

## Rides

- **A ride is its own kind of edit** (one per trip, next to the edits of each length): the whole trip from one camera in time order, with stops cut out and the rest sped up. It shares revisions, music, the mix, rendering and export with the other edits, and has its own settings (speed or length, camera, corrections), saved with each revision.
- **Stops come from GPS speed** when the camera recorded it: GoPro telemetry (GPS5, GPS9, with fix and DOP) and dashcams that write one text line per second into a metadata track (checked on a dashcam whose line carries speed in knots). Below 1.5 km/h for at least 6 s is a stop, above 3 km/h is travel; a second of each stop's edges stays, so the car is seen slowing down and setting off.
- **Without GPS, from the picture:** optical flow on a 4x4 grid of tiles; the busiest tiles (85th percentile) of those that ever move, smoothed over 5 s. Not the whole picture's motion: on an open road most of the frame (sky, far road, smooth asphalt) barely moves even at speed, and a turn moves everything. Thresholds come from each camera's own footage, and only clear stops are cut: everything uncertain is kept. Checked against GPS on 60 recordings (2.9 h) of the dashcam day: no driving called a stop, 65% of the stopped time found.
- **One speed per ride:** fixed, or fitted exactly to the length (for 5.3 h of driving into 10 minutes, 32.1x). The app lists the usual speeds with the length each gives. A length the route can't fit even at 64x is refused with the length it needs; nothing is dropped behind your back.
- **Corrections:** drop a stretch, or keep a stop at normal speed; stops kept at normal speed are where people talk or something happens. Drops from every edit apply to the ride too.
- **Sped-up rendering** keeps one frame grid along each stretch (chapters and 120 s render chunks start on it), samples the first frame exactly (`settb=AVTB,setpts=PTS/s,fps=..:round=up`, before scaling and colour conversion), and speeds up the sound with `atempo` in steps of 2x. Every source frame is still decoded: the dashcam day (5.5 h of 1080p HEVC) renders as a 10-minute ride in about 13 minutes on an RTX 5090.
- **Sound:** a ride starts with the music at 0 dB and the clips' own sound at −20 dB (sped-up sound is mostly noise); the clip sound level goes down to off. Sped-up speech doesn't duck the music. No beat fitting for rides.
- **Export:** OpenTimelineIO `LinearTimeWarp` and FCPXML `timeMap` on each sped-up clip; muted clip sound is exported muted. The retiming hasn't been checked in DaVinci Resolve yet.

## Editor export

- **Two formats from one plan:** FCPXML 1.10 (DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro import it) and OpenTimelineIO through the official library.
- **Basic cuts only:** which frames of which original go where, with each clip's own sound. The edit's music comes as a WAV on its own audio track, at its level and ducked under speech; the clips' sound isn't lowered under it, and loudness, crop positions, photo zooms and color conversion are not exported.
- **Clips reference the originals and their source timecode;** files without timecode start at zero. Drop-frame timecode is handled for 29.97 and 59.94 fps. Timeline positions use the same frame counts as the render, so both cut at the same frames.
