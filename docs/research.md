# No tool yet cuts your ride three ways

*A survey of existing tools, written in October 2026 before Reelsift was started. It uses one example throughout: four hours of footage from a bike ride with two cameras, to be cut into a 30-second, a 3-minute and a 10-minute edit.*

As of October 2026, **no single product does the whole job**. None takes 4 hours of multi-camera cycling footage, accepts "more scenery and descents, less flat road and stops", produces nested ~30 s, ~3 min and ~10 min edits, and shows you the chosen segments to veto before rendering. Some products cover parts of it. **Adobe Firefly Quick Cut** has the exact duration presets and takes a text prompt, but it is a beta built mainly around talking footage. **Eddie AI v4** handles 100 hours of input and exports cleanly to every major NLE, but its new visual montage mode has no evidence behind it yet for footage with no dialogue. The camera-vendor apps (Insta360, GoPro Quik) understand action footage but stop at about an hour of input, one short cut, and no way to describe what you want. The closest match is a small open-source project, **ride-recap** (July 2026). It fuses GoPro and Garmin telemetry with a per-clip vision-model scan and stops at a select/reject review screen before composing. It is GoPro-only, outputs only 30 s and 60 s reels, and has no free-text preference input. Reported quality is thin everywhere: no independent review tests any of these tools on long, wordless action footage. The building blocks, by contrast, are mature and cheap: a hybrid pipeline costs **roughly $1–5 per 4-hour trip** in cloud API fees, or nothing beyond hardware when run locally. A custom pipeline is warranted if you will run it on more than a handful of trips and are comfortable extending a Python codebase. The best route is to fork or reimplement ride-recap's design and add multi-camera sync, a text-to-weights preference layer, nested duration tiers, and NLE export.

## Camera apps understand bikes but stop at an hour

The camera-ecosystem apps are the only consumer tools tuned for action footage, and their hard limits rule them out for a 4-hour trip. The **Insta360 app's Auto Edit** has the highest documented ceiling: **100 files and 60 minutes total** on iPhone 12-class or newer phones, and under 20 minutes on other devices. It mixes 360, flat and cloud media and uses scene recognition and music matching. Its first generated video is capped at about **3 minutes**, and duration options shrink if the AI finds few highlights ([Insta360 manual](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/app/en-us/operation-tutorial/edit-function/auto-edit)). Analysis runs on the phone, so the phone's compute is what limits input length. **GoPro Quik's Auto Highlights** allow at most **75 items**, treat videos over 20 minutes as unsupported, and need footage captured within a 12-hour window ([GoPro Support](https://community.gopro.com/s/article/GoPro-Quik-Auto-Highlight-Videos?language=en_US)). Quik's themed mobile edits come in 15, 30 or 60 seconds, and Quik for Desktop caps edits at **60 seconds** ([projectgo.pro](https://projectgo.pro/how-long-can-gopro-quik-videos-be/)). DJI LightCut matches clips to templates and reportedly recognizes cycling as a theme, but publishes no input limits ([DJI LightCut](https://www.dji.com/lightcut); [DroneDJ](https://dronedj.com/2023/02/23/dji-video-editing-app-lightcut/)).

None of these apps accepts a preference about content. You steer them only through template, music, filter and length. The gallery AIs do accept natural language: Apple Memories takes a text description ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-gu/guide/iphone/iphf7de217f0/ios)) and Google Photos Highlight video takes a search query ([Google Photos Help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128862)). Both choose *which assets* to include, not which seconds inside a long helmet-cam file. **No consumer app found uses GPS speed or elevation**, which is the most direct way to find descents. All of them generate first and let you edit the timeline afterwards, and all export only a finished MP4. Reviewers call Quik's auto edits "basic" ([Digital Camera World](https://digitalcameraworld.com/reviews/gopro-quik-app-review)) and say it "gets you 80% there" while sometimes picking a boring segment over an epic one ([ActionCamLab](https://actioncamlab.com/mobile-video-editing-apps-compared)). Google Photos is criticized for not always picking the best footage ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/google-photos-highlight-video)). Insta360 FlashCut fails outright when it can't find enough usable clips ([Insta360](https://www.insta360.com/support/supportcourse?post_id=13826)). With these apps, a 4-hour trip means splitting it into several 60-minute or 75-item batches. Each batch is selected independently, so you get no nested multi-length set.

## Pro AI editors have prompts and durations but think in transcripts

Most AI editors and clippers pick moments from the transcript. Descript, Gling, Vizard, Munch, Kapwing, Wisecut, Pictory, FireCut and (per its own docs) ChatCut fall in this group. They are a poor fit for footage that is mostly wind noise and scenery. ChatCut's documentation says its analysis is "transcription-based" and that visual analysis is "coming soon" ([ChatCut Docs](https://chatcut.io/docs/what-is-chatcut)). Descript's agent is called weak at montages because there is "no transcript for the agent to work from" ([Motionbox](https://motionbox.io/blog/agent-that-edits-video-2026)). The pro NLEs added visual *search* in 2026, not automatic selection. Premiere's media intelligence, DaVinci Resolve 21's IntelliSearch ([Broadcast Now](https://www.broadcastnow.co.uk/tech-innovation/blackmagic-adds-ai-search-and-voice-creation-in-davinci-resolve-21/5216037.article)) and Final Cut Pro 12's Visual Search all fall here. FCP 12 indexed **9.5 hours in just under 2 hours** on-device, but matching is fuzzy: "sea" missed clips that "ocean" found ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/whats-new-in-final-cut-pro-12-and-more/)). These tools speed up a human editor; none assembles a highlight edit. Final Cut Pro for iPad's Montage Maker does build an edit from "the best visual moments", but it accepts no steering ([Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/photography/video-editing/final-cut-pro-will-soon-search-with-ai-detect-beats-and-automate-a-montage-on-the-heels-of-the-apple-creator-studio-announcement)).

Three commercial products come close. **Firefly Quick Cut** (beta since February 2026) takes a plain-language description such as "travel vlog", plus an optional shot list. It detects scene changes, color-corrects, and builds a multitrack first edit ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/adobes-new-firefly-quick-cut-tool-turns-raw-footage-into-a-rough-edit-from-a-text-prompt/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/25/adobe-firefly-can-now-use-ai-to-turn-raw-footage-into-a-first-cut-video/)). Its duration dropdown runs **Auto, 30 s, 60 s, 90 s, 3 min, 5 min, 10 min, 15 min**, which maps directly onto the brief ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/firefly-video-editor/create-quick-cut/create-video-quick-cut.html)). Each file must be **under 20 GB and at most 4 hours** ([Adobe known limitations](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/firefly-video-editor/troubleshoot/known-limitations-in-firefly-video-editor.html)), so raw action-cam files need transcoding before upload. **Eddie AI v4** (September 2026) accepts **up to 100 hours**. It uploads only low-res proxies and keeps full-res media local. It added B-roll montages, beat-cutting and reference edits, and exports to Premiere, Resolve, Final Cut, Avid and OTIO with relinking to originals. Pricing is credits: import costs **at most $2.50 per source hour**, about $10 for this trip, and new accounts get 1,000 free credits ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/eddie-ai-v4-explained-visual-b-roll-edits-reference-edits-an-agent-that-joins-your-nle-project/); [Eddie Help](https://www.heyeddie.ai/help/what-can-i-ask-for)). Eddie's core job is still described as cutting interviews and covering them with B-roll, and no user report on dialogue-free montages exists. **OpusClip ClipAnything** genuinely analyzes visuals, audio and sentiment and lists "videos with little to no dialogue" as a use case ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/clipanything)). But it outputs separate clips rather than one cohesive edit, takes single files up to 10 GB ([Opus help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/local-file-uploads)), and admits that, while in beta, it may return nothing for videos with little audio or visual activity ([Opus help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/clipanything-qa-2)).

No commercial product offers the review step as specified: a list of selected segments where you untick some and the edit re-fills to its target length. Timeline editing after generation (Quick Cut, invideo, Mosaic) and conversational revision (Eddie) are the closest substitutes. Running Quick Cut three times at 30 s, 3 min and 10 min yields three independent selections, with no guarantee that the short cut is a subset of the long one.

## A hobbyist repo already proves the design works

**ride-recap** is the most instructive find because it was built for exactly this problem. It takes "hours of raw GoPro footage + a `.fit` file" and gathers candidate moments from four sources: Garmin telemetry (speed, heart rate, power, climbs), Strava popular segments, a vision-model rating of proxy frames, and optional hand labels. An LLM "narrative pass" picks about 20 clips. A redundancy penalty then combines time distance with kinematic similarity and DINOv2 visual similarity, which lets it tell a climb from the descent off the same summit. A `--coverage-weight` dial trades best-moments-only against whole-ride coverage. The `process` step stops at a **Streamlit reviewer with 5-second previews**, and the final reel is rendered only from the clips you selected ([ride-recap README](https://github.com/ianmacomber/ride-recap)). It scans GoPro's 848×480 `.LRV` proxies, which the README says cuts frame extraction from about 25 minutes to about 30 seconds. It runs on Gemini, OpenAI, or a local Qwen3-VL-8B via mlx-vlm.

The author's write-up is the best field evidence on quality. **Prompt iteration mattered more than model choice**: the scan prompt went through 9 versions in 3 months and grew from 20 to 244 lines. Telemetry alone misled the model until athlete-specific power zones were injected. Gemini overrated tunnels, picked adjacent clips, and returned about 10 clips when asked for 20 ([Teaching LLMs Taste](https://iandmacomber.com/blog/gopro-garmin-gemini-ride-recap)). The cost figures don't reconcile. The README claims "roughly four cents" per ride, but the blog reports **about $60 to scan 180,430 seconds across 25 rides**, about $2.40 per ride, which likely includes development iterations. The project has 15 stars and a custom license, and one Hacker News commenter said the sample reel looked like "random samples" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48957639)). Treat it as a reference design, not a finished product.

The rest of the open-source landscape splits in two. Local highlight apps such as **VideoHighlighter** (AGPL-3.0) score scene, motion, audio and objects and show *why* each moment scored ([README](https://github.com/wj4616/VideoHighlighter)). **edit-mind** indexed 669 GB of GoPro footage with Qwen2.5-VL and pushes selected clips to a DaVinci Resolve timeline ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48528029)), though it describes itself as not production-ready ([edit-mind](https://github.com/IliasHad/edit-mind)). The high-star agent editors are built around speech. video-use (29k stars) states "The LLM never watches the video. It reads it." ([video-use](https://github.com/browser-use/video-use)). On wordless cycling footage, these editors are missing their main cutting signal.

## Cheap video models can score segments but can't place cuts

The model layer is cheap enough that price never decides the architecture. **Gemini** is still the only frontier API that ingests video natively. It costs about **300 tokens per second at default resolution or about 100 at low resolution**, and a 1M-token context holds about 1 hour (default) or 3 hours (low), so 4 hours needs several requests ([Gemini video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)). At Gemini 3.8 Flash's **$0.75 per million input tokens, which doubles to $1.50 on January 1, 2027** ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)), the whole trip costs about $3.24 to ingest at default resolution. **Twelve Labs** is the most video-native managed service. Marengo 3.0 indexes up to 4 hours per call, and Pegasus 1.5 returns custom-schema segment metadata with segments as short as 2 seconds. Index plus analysis costs about $18–19 for the trip ([Twelve Labs](https://www.twelvelabs.io/llms-full.txt); [segmentation blog](https://www.twelvelabs.io/blog/video-segmentation-api-how-to-extract-structured-data-from-video)). OpenAI and Anthropic take only frames as images. Claude allows up to 600 images per request, so 7,200 sampled frames would cost about $3 on Haiku 4.5 ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)).

Accuracy is the real constraint. On **ExtremeWhenBench** (hour-scale videos, mean length 75.7 minutes), Gemini 3.5 Flash scores **0.115 mIoU** against 0.466 on short Charades-STA clips. Plain **CLIP frame retrieval at 1 fps beats every video-LLM tested, at 0.269**. A hybrid that has CLIP retrieve the top 3 windows and then lets Qwen3.5-9B ground within them reaches **0.354**, 6.7 times the single-model approach. The authors attribute 85% of failures to *search* rather than recognition ([arXiv 2606.12300](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12300)). GPT-5.4 had a 50.7% output-parse failure rate on the same task. Practitioners have reached the same conclusion: imgly's videoclipper has the LLM return transcript text and maps it back to ASR word timestamps, because model-emitted timecodes were unreliable ([imgly/videoclipper](https://github.com/imgly/videoclipper)). In practice, the code should decide where cuts go and the model should decide what is good. Ask a VLM to describe and score a short, pre-cut window, never to "watch four hours and give me timestamps." Egocentric footage is harder still: Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 20.45 mIoU on Ego4D-NLQ versus 39.28 on Charades-STA ([NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/5d2e24df9cfaad3189833b819c40b392-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Dedicated highlight-detection models are research-grade. They were trained on third-person YouTube vlogs, and their licenses are often non-standard. **Lighthouse** is the cleanest way to try one as an extra query-conditioned signal ([line/lighthouse](https://github.com/line/lighthouse)).

## The gap is integration, so build the pipeline

The market gap is specific. No product combines (1) multi-camera ingest synced on one clock, (2) telemetry-aware scoring that knows a descent from a flat road, (3) free-text preferences compiled into scoring weights, (4) **nested** duration tiers selected from a single analysis, (5) a pre-render segment veto that re-fills to the target length, and (6) NLE export. Each piece exists somewhere: telemetry fusion and pre-compose review in ride-recap, the duration presets in Quick Cut, multi-hour proxy ingest and NLE export in Eddie. Nobody has integrated them, and no surveyed project turns a sentence like "more scenery, less stops" into weights. Two smaller gaps matter for this trip: no open-source tool syncs phone footage (which has no embedded telemetry) to a GPX/FIT track, and none auto-reframes Insta360 360° footage for highlights.

A custom pipeline is warranted for a repeat use case. The expensive step, scanning the footage, happens once per trip, and every later change (a new preference, a different length, a vetoed segment) is a cheap re-selection over a stored segment table. Commercial tools force a full re-run for each of those. For a one-off trip, the pragmatic alternative is Quick Cut run three times, or Eddie if you finish in an NLE, followed by manual trimming. The recommended architecture follows ride-recap's candidate → score → select → review → render design, extended where the brief requires.

| Stage | Components | Design rule |
|---|---|---|
| Ingest and sync | [telemetry-parser](https://github.com/AdrianEddy/telemetry-parser) (GoPro, Insta360, DJI, phone sensor apps), [gpxpy](https://github.com/tkrajina/gpxpy) / [fitdecode](https://github.com/polyvertex/fitdecode), file creation times for phone clips; 720p or `.LRV` proxies; Insta360 Studio Auto Frame to pre-reframe 360 ([manual](https://onlinemanual.insta360.com/studio/en-us/operation-guide/edit-function/auto-frame)) | Correct clock drift explicitly; GoPro drifts "a few seconds per week" without sync ([blog](https://iandmacomber.com/blog/gopro-garmin-gemini-ride-recap)) |
| Segment | [PySceneDetect](https://github.com/Breakthrough/PySceneDetect) plus fixed 3–10 s windows, IDs per source file | Continuous POV footage has few hard cuts; never ask a model for global timestamps |
| Dense cheap signals | GPS speed/grade (descent, stopped), SigLIP/CLIP at 1 fps against prompt banks, [silero-vad](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad)/[faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper) for conversation, [DINOv2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2) for redundancy | Telemetry decides descent/flat/stop; vision decides scenery |
| VLM rubric | Top 20–40% of windows to Gemini 3.8 Flash or 3.5 Flash-Lite, or local [Qwen3-VL-8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct); strict JSON scores | Short windows only, where grounding is reliable |
| Preference compiler | One LLM call maps free text to a weight vector and hard filters over the stored features | Re-select without re-scanning |
| Select | Greedy ranking with redundancy and coverage penalties per budget; build 10 min first, then 3 min and 30 s as subsets | Code sums durations and snaps cuts to boundaries |
| Review | Web storyboard with thumbnails and keep/reject/must-include; precedents in ride-recap and [LosslessCut](https://github.com/mifi/lossless-cut) | Vetoes trigger re-fill to target length |
| Render/export | FFmpeg or [MoviePy](https://github.com/Zulko/moviepy); [OpenTimelineIO](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenTimelineIO) adapters, [auto-editor](https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor)-style Premiere/Resolve/FCP export, or [davinci-resolve-mcp](https://github.com/samuelgursky/davinci-resolve-mcp) | Render from full-res originals, analyze proxies |

Per-trip cost depends mostly on where the VLM runs. A **local-only** build (CLIP plus Qwen3-VL-8B) costs nothing at the margin but needs a GPU with about 24 GB of memory. Local per-window captioning probably takes 1–3 hours of wall-clock time, an unverified estimate. The **hybrid cloud** build sends about 1.5 hours of candidate windows to Gemini 3.8 Flash for about $1.20, plus a few cents of text-only editor calls. That is **under $2 per trip at 2026 rates and roughly $3 after Gemini's January 2027 price change**. ride-recap's real-world spend of about $2.40 per ride, development included, brackets this nicely. Ingesting the whole video into Gemini instead costs about $0.45–3.25, depending on model and resolution. The managed **Twelve Labs** route costs about $18–19 but replaces the segmentation and rubric code with one schema-driven call. All of these undercut a month of most commercial subscriptions.

## Conclusion

The models are no longer the hard part of a personal highlight reel. Taste and timing are. ride-recap's 244-line prompt, and the benchmark result that cheap CLIP retrieval beats frontier video-LLMs at finding moments in long footage, point the same way: quality comes from deterministic segmentation, domain signals such as speed and gradient that vision models cannot infer reliably, and a rubric tuned on your own reactions. A bigger model won't substitute for those. Commercial vendors are converging on visual montage features (Eddie v4, Quick Cut, ClipAnything), so expect the prompt-plus-duration half of this brief to become a commodity within a year. The telemetry-aware, nested-tier, veto-and-refill half is unlikely to arrive from a vendor any time soon, because it serves a niche that sits between action-cam apps and pro NLEs.

The decision that matters most is to treat the segment table as the product, not the rendered video. Once every window of the trip is scored and stored, the three edits, any later preference change, and every veto become sub-second re-selections. That is something no current product does, and it is why building is justified here. Two things to validate before committing: whether the forkable code's custom license permits reuse, and whether a vision rubric scores your 360° and phone footage as well as it scores GoPro proxies. Neither has been tested publicly.
